START WITH THIS NUMBER

What fraction of your transcripts contain any PII?

Call it p. It decides the architecture on its own, because p is exactly how often the speculative summary is thrown away and paid for twice. Everything else on this page is a consequence of it.

9 / 10transcripts in the labelled set triggered a detection

The one that did not is an artificial construct — a troubleshooting call in which nobody says a name. In practice a customer's name alone makes a transcript dirty, and over-redaction compounds it: a permitted "card ending in 4242" reference is enough by itself. For PCI-scoped call transcripts, expect p between 0.9 and 1.0.

High prevalence (p > ~0.6) — the call-centre case

Use sequential: redact, then summarize the redacted transcript. Parallel costs 60–67% more and buys only 70–300 ms. It also happens to be the only design that keeps un-redacted text away from the summarizer, so cost and privacy point the same way.

Low prevalence (p < ~0.3) — mostly-clean corpora

Use speculative parallel. The extra summarization is charged only on transcripts that actually contain PII, so at p = 0.1 you pay $200 per million to remove 587 hours of aggregate latency. That is an excellent trade — provided un-redacted text may reach the summarizer at all.

THE TRADE

What a second of latency costs, at each prevalence

Sequential is flat: $0.0030 and 4,617 ms per transcript no matter what. Parallel is $0.0030 + p × $0.0020 and 2,277 + p × 2,270 ms. Neither dominates — parallel always wins on latency, sequential always wins on cost — so the question is only ever what the latency is worth.

EVIDENCE · LATENCY

Parallel is never slower — that part holds

Conversation PII and conversation summarization take almost exactly the same time on this resource (~2.27 s each), so the speculative summary runs entirely inside the shadow of the PII call and costs nothing in latency. That is why parallel ties rather than loses on dirty transcripts. It is a real result — it just is not worth much when almost every transcript is dirty.

EVIDENCE · REDACTION QUALITY

Does it catch what it needs to?

Ten hand-labelled transcripts, 41 labelled spans, scored on whether a labelled value survived verbatim in the redacted output — the actual requirement, not span overlap.

Spoken-digit card numbersDetected
  • Dates of birth are not redacted on the GA API. DateOfBirth is preview-only and is rejected outright as a category value on 2024-11-01. An identity-verification call that reads a date of birth aloud leaks it. Opting into 2025-11-15-preview fixes it at confidence 1.0, at the price of accepting Microsoft's preview terms.
  • Spoken digits are handled. "four one one one, one one one one…" is caught as CreditCard, as are spoken expiry dates. A speech pipeline without inverse text normalisation does not defeat detection — the main worry going in, and it did not materialise.
  • Last-four references are over-redacted, and this inflates p. "the card ending in 4242" is masked despite PCI DSS permitting it. Because any detection makes a transcript dirty, a permitted reference alone pushes it into the expensive branch of the parallel design.
  • Short turns near card numbers get swallowed. Agent backchannels like "Got it." and "Mhm." were labelled CreditCard at 0.74–0.86 confidence and fully masked. Nothing leaked, but six of nine turns disappeared, leaving the summarizer far less to work with. A confidence floor on short turns would recover most of it.
  • Never send isolated utterances. One turn with no surrounding context redacts badly — a surname survives and a card number is only partially masked, which looks redacted but is not. Send whole conversations.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS

Honest limits

  • Measure your own p before acting on any of this. The 9-in-10 figure comes from ten transcripts written to probe failure modes, not from production traffic. One /api/redact call per transcript over a real sample settles it, and it is the number the whole recommendation turns on.
  • Both fixtures are ~600 characters. Real transcripts are far longer, and summarization time grows with input while the PII call may not — if the two stop being equal, the speculative summary stops being free and sequential wins by more. Re-run this at production length.
  • Ten synthetic transcripts is enough to find categorical gaps like the date-of-birth one. It is not enough to estimate a leak rate, and it says nothing about how common each pattern is in real traffic.
  • All measurements come from one Standard-tier resource in eastus2 under no competing load. Latency under production concurrency, and across the three regions in use, is unmeasured.
